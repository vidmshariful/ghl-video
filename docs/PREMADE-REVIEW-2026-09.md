# Premade, reviewed from the data: 16 September 2026

The first of three deep dives, one per production part. This one is Premade:
what the product is, what the real orders show about how the studio actually
works, what the client sees, and where the handling should change. The
studio screens, the client portal, the brief form, the emails, the HighLevel
mirror and the code behind all of it were read for this; the numbers come
from production (read only). Nothing was changed.

## 1. What Premade is

A premade video is a finished template from the library, made the client's
own: logo and brand colours on every frame, their platform screenshots
dropped in, the voiceover re-recorded to say their brand name, optionally
tailored to their niche. The catalog holds 66 videos in five families (full
explainer, feature explainer, demo, marketing, feature animation), 5 packs
and 11 bundles. A single video is $97 to $995. The AI First SaaS Pack is 9
videos for $1,995. Bundles let the client pick videos on the brief.

The promise on the site: delivered in 5 to 7 days, counted from the brief,
not from payment. One revision round included. Full commercial rights.

The handbook's intended flow, in six steps: they pay, they send the brief,
we build (each video set to In production), we send each video (paste the
link, set Ready to review, the client is emailed), they review each video
(notes at the second, approve or request changes), it finishes itself when
the last video is approved.

## 2. What the real orders show

Production has 6 premade orders with real money, all from late July to
August 2026. Small, but they tell the whole story.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 28 Jul | speedmobi | AI First pack, 9 videos | $1,397 | in the record, flag never set | 39 days | 4 approved by us, 4 with the client, 1 in production |
| 30 Jul | madcapper | AI First pack, 9 videos | $1,397 | 20 days after paying | 24 days | all 9 sent back with the same note, no new cut, 19 notes unanswered |
| 30 Jul | colelab | AI First pack, 9 videos | $998 | never through the site | 37 days | 4 approved, 5 with the client |

| 26 Aug | a $1 test order | marketing video | $1 | no | none | paid, nothing built |

Six things stand out.

**The brief is not where the work starts.** Three of the four pack clients
never sent a brief through the site, and the studio built their videos
anyway, from assets that came by email or from the client's website. The
tool says "Paid, brief in, nothing built" is the only way into the queue.
The studio does not work that way, so the queue does not show the work.

**One brief is in the record and the system does not know.** The speedmobi
brief was submitted on 28 July (logo, colours, pronunciation, five
screenshots), but the order's "brief received" flag never flipped. The
studio saw "Waiting on brief", no due dates were ever set, and the client
was sent a reminder for a brief he had already sent.

**The studio confirms the brand by email, then builds in batches.** The
updates the producer posted say it plainly: "An email has been sent to
confirm if the videos are for myleadhub.com", "Branding isn't confirmed
yet", "We got the confirmation, started working on it", "First 4 videos
have been created", "The branding colours have been fixed". Confirm which
company and which assets, build in batches of four, fix what the client
flags, then the rest. Each video carries its own branding and colour scheme,
so the batches are not one brand pass repeated. The platform has no step
for confirming the brand and no idea of a batch; it only knows nine separate
videos.

**A pack takes five weeks to a first cut, against a seven day promise.** 24
to 39 days from payment to the first cut on every pack. Part of that is the
brief arriving late or not at all; part of it is that the promise is seven
days for a nine video pack, the same as for one video, because the pack
carries no delivery days of its own.

**One wrong logo became eighteen notes and nine burned revision rounds.**
The madcapper client wrote the same note on every video: the logo used was
an old one from the website, not the one in the brand kit. Eighteen notes
across nine videos, one cause. Each "request changes" spent that video's
one included round, so the client is now told on every video that his round
is used, for a mistake that was ours. Nineteen of those notes have no reply
from us, and no new cut has gone up.

**Videos sit with the client for weeks and nobody chased.** Fifteen videos
have been "ready" with clients for two to six weeks. No reminder ever went
out before 15 September because the scheduled jobs had never run. On one
order the studio approved four videos itself a minute after marking them
ready, which the handbook allows when a client goes quiet.

The mechanics the studio does use are solid: 41 cuts uploaded, 93 status
changes, 12 updates posted to clients, all through the job page. The work
happens in the platform. It is the shape around the work that is wrong.

## 3. What the client sees

The client's side is in better shape than the studio's, and most of it
should stay.

- **Dashboard:** a three step "Getting started" (add your brand, send your
  brief, approve your first video), three counters (Ready to watch, Waiting
  on you, In progress), "Coming up" with expected dates, recent orders.
- **Pre-made:** every video they own with a still, its status in plain words
  and a due date. "Watch and review" opens the popup: player, notes pinned
  to a second, Approve this video, Request changes, Download.
- **The order page:** a five pip progress bar (Paid, Intake, In production,
  Review, Delivered), the brief card with "View or update brief", a delivery
  card, "Your producer" with Message the studio, Email, WhatsApp and Book a
  call, the video list with status chips, and the updates the producer
  posted.
- **The brief:** brand name, how it is pronounced, two colours, logo, up to
  eight platform screenshots, notes. Pre-filled from the Brand Kit on a
  second order.
- **Emails:** confirmation with the brief link, brief received, one "video
  ready" per video, replies, a reminder after three days, the wrap-up when
  the last video is approved.

What is wrong on their side:

- A nine video pack sends nine "video ready" emails.
- The included round is counted per video, so a pack client has nine rounds
  to keep track of, and a studio mistake spends them all.
- The order page's delivery card still talks about PlayBook and says
  "Your finished files will appear here" on an order that is delivered.
- The progress bar has an "Intake" pip nothing can reach, so it jumps from
  Paid to In production.
- The WhatsApp button has no phone number behind it.
- A queued video, and every row under "Coming up", has no button at all.
- A premade video cannot be shared by link; custom and editing videos can.
- Editing the Brand Kit does not reach an order already briefed, and nothing
  tells the client that. The screenshots they sent cannot be viewed or
  removed one at a time.
- The site promises a voiceover accent choice and niche tailoring; the brief
  asks for neither. The studio asks for the website by email; the brief does
  not ask for it.

## 4. What the studio sees

Premade in admin is "What needs us" (Answer the client, Changes to make,
Ready to start, With the client), "The board" (five columns of orders, moved
with arrows), and the job page (owner, order stage, one row per video with a
status dropdown, a link field and Save, "Set all to", the brief, the client
updates).

What is wrong on this side, beyond what the earlier review covered:

- A video with no brief in the system is invisible to the queue, though the
  studio builds those videos anyway.
- There is no step for confirming the brand before building, which is the
  step the studio does by email on every pack.
- Nine link fields with nine Saves for a pack; no way to paste a batch.
- The studio cannot set or move a due date; it comes from the brief or not
  at all, and slips show in red on the client's screen with no way to
  re-promise.
- Moving a video to "revisions" from our side spends the client's round, and
  there is no way to give a round back.
- Every order defaults to one hardcoded producer name.
- The order stage is a second ladder nobody uses (five of six orders never
  had it set by hand), with an "Intake" step that cannot be reached and a
  "deliver" that exists on the board and is refused on the job page.
- Brief reminders stop after 14 days, and stop for good if a producer moves
  the order along, with no list of "paid, no brief, out of the window".
- Nineteen client notes are unanswered and there is no reply box on the
  queue row; answering means opening the job, finding the video, opening
  its notes.

## 5. How Premade should be managed

Nothing here is a new feature. It is the studio's real rhythm written into
the screens that exist, with the gaps filled.

**The unit of work is the order, and the order is one brand applied to N
videos.** Premade is the one line where that is true, so the Premade board
stays a board of orders, and the job page stays one page per order.

**The flow on the job page, top to bottom, in the order the studio does
it:**

1. **The brand.** The brief as it is today (logo, colours, pronunciation,
   screenshots, notes) plus the three things the studio asks for anyway:
   the website, the voiceover accent, the niche when they paid for it. One
   tick, "Brand confirmed", by the producer. If the brief came by email, the
   producer fills it in here on the client's behalf, and that counts as
   received. The brief flag is read from the record, so the speedmobi case
   cannot happen again.
2. **The videos.** Every video in a pack carries its own branding and
   colour scheme, so there is no single brand pass to approve first
   (Shariful, 16 September). Videos go to the client as each is ready or
   all at once: paste the links in one box, one per line, and "Send N
   videos" as one email.
3. **The review.** Notes and replies on the job page itself, with an
   "applies to every video" switch the client can set on a note, so a
   wrong logo is one note and one fix while colour notes stay per video.
4. **Done.** When the last video is approved, the order closes itself, as
   it does now. A client who goes quiet for a week after the second nudge
   is approved on their behalf and told why.

**The board's columns are the flow, derived, never set by hand:** Waiting
on brief, Ready to start, Building, With the client, Done. Paid with no
brief is Waiting on brief. Paid with a brief is Ready to start. The arrows
go. The stage the client sees on their order page becomes the same five
words.

**"What needs us" stays the default view and gains what the data says is
missing:** a "No brief" bucket with a nudge button and "enter it for them",
a reply box on "Answer the client" rows, and a "Nudge" and "Approve for
them" on the oldest "With the client" rows, with the two nudge rule the
handbook already states.

**Dates the studio owns.** A pack promises 14 days from the brief; single
videos keep 5 to 7. The producer can move a date with one line the client
sees as an update.

**Revisions counted the way the client experiences them.** One round per
order, and a status move made by the studio never spends it.

**The client side keeps its shape.** Fix the stale delivery card, the empty
WhatsApp link, the unreachable "Intake" pip, the buttons missing on queued
and "Coming up" rows, share links for premade, one "ready" email per batch,
and a Brand Kit edit that offers to update the open orders.

**HighLevel stays as it is:** contact, tags, the paid invoice, the
"waiting on" field that a workflow can chase from. Premade work lives on
our board.

## 6. Decisions, 16 September 2026

1. **The pack's promise: 14 days from the brief.** Single videos keep 5
   to 7 days.
2. **No brand first step.** Every video in a pack has its own branding
   and colour scheme, so videos go out as each is ready or all at once,
   never one first for approval. The batch email and the "applies to every
   video" note stay.
3. **One revision round per order.** A studio move never spends it.
4. **Quiet clients are approved on their behalf** after two nudges and a
   week, and told why.
5. **Briefs by email: yes.** The producer may enter a brief the client
   sent by email; it counts as received and the clock starts.

## 7. Order of work, once decided

- **First, the defects (a day), built 16 September and proven on staging:**
  the brief flag read from the record, with the speedmobi order corrected;
  the delivery card, WhatsApp link, Intake pip and missing buttons on the
  client side; one "ready" email per batch; the round counted per order and
  never spent by a studio move; the pack's 14 day promise. Live when the
  owner says so.
- **Second, the brief (one to two days):** website, accent and niche
  fields; enter it for them; Brand Kit edits reaching open orders;
  screenshots viewable one by one.
- **Third, the job page (two to three days):** the brand block with its
  tick, batch link paste and "Send N videos", notes with replies and
  "applies to all", the date the producer can move, the producer picked
  from the team, approve on their behalf after the second nudge and a
  week.
- **Fourth, the board and the queue (two days):** derived columns, no
  arrows, the "No brief" bucket, nudge and approve-for-them on the queue.

Each step goes to staging first and is walked through, then live when the
owner says so.
